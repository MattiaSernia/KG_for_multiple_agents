Public Tender - Centre Hospitalier Régional de Belval (CHRB)
Implementation of an AI assistance solution for hospital staff
1. Context and objectives
The Centre Hospitalier Régional de Belval (CHRB) is a public regional hospital serving the Belval region (approximately 1,200 beds and 4,500 staff across medical, care and medico-administrative functions). As part of its digital transformation and of the national Ségur du numérique en santé programme, CHRB wishes to improve the efficiency of its staff and the quality and safety of the care delivered to patients.
Staff face a growing complexity of procedures, a multiplicity of information sources (care protocols, recommendations from the Haute Autorité de Santé (HAS), internal procedures, regulatory texts, business systems) and an increasing volume of administrative and documentation work that reduces the time available for patient care.
In this context, CHRB wishes to procure, deploy and operate an artificial intelligence solution that assists staff in their daily activities - facilitating access to information, improving the quality of written outputs, and reducing processing and documentation times - while remaining strictly a non-decisional support tool.
The present contract covers the design, implementation and deployment of such a solution.
2. General description of the need
The expected solution must provide hospital staff with an intelligent assistance tool, accessible from their work environment, capable of supporting them in their clinical-administrative activities. This assistance may notably cover:
retrieval of information from internal procedures, care protocols and applicable regulation;
assistance in drafting medical and administrative documents;
support in handling dossiers (admissions, coding, medico-social pathways, etc.);
support for the continuity of care between professionals, shifts and services.
The solution must fit into the existing practices of staff and must not introduce a rupture in clinical or administrative workflows.
3. Functional scope
3.1 Information consultation assistance. The solution must let staff quickly access reliable, up-to-date information drawn from the authority's internal procedures, care protocols and HAS recommendations, applicable regulatory texts, and internal documents (guides, notes, reference frameworks, drug references). It must contextualise its answers to the staff member's request.
3.2 Drafting assistance. The solution must assist the drafting of clinical and administrative content, notably discharge summaries and medical reports (comptes-rendus), medical correspondence and patient letters, administrative documents and dossier syntheses. Generated content must be adapted to the clinical/administrative context and conform to expected quality standards.
3.3 Dossier-handling assistance. The solution must assist staff in analysing and handling dossiers by surfacing relevant information, recalling applicable rules, and suggesting actions or verifications. It must support medical coding (PMSI / ICD-10) for activity reporting (T2A). It must take the dossier context into account where available. No decision may be automated.
3.4 Collaboration and continuity of care. The solution must facilitate the continuity of care between professionals and services by enabling relevant information sharing, an understanding of the context of an in-progress patient pathway, and an efficient resumption of handling by another professional.
4. Regulatory and legal constraints
4.1 Personal and health data protection. The titleholder must guarantee strict compliance with the GDPR. Health data is processed as special-category data (GDPR Art. 9) and medical confidentiality (secret médical) must be preserved. Data must be hosted exclusively within the European Union; no transfer of data outside the EU is authorised; sensitive data must be appropriately protected (encryption, pseudonymisation where relevant).
4.2 Health-data hosting (HDS). The infrastructure hosting the solution must hold the French Hébergeur de Données de Santé (HDS) certification.
4.3 Explainability and traceability. Recommendations or suggestions produced by the solution must be explainable. It must be possible, a posteriori, to understand the elements that led to an output (source grounding, audit trail), consistent with HAS guidance on the use of AI in healthcare.
4.4 Medical responsibility. The solution must be designed as an assistance tool. No clinical or medico-administrative decision may be automated without explicit validation by a qualified professional. The solution must be positioned so as not to require medical-device qualification (CE marking / MDR); should any use case approach that threshold, the bidder must explicitly address the implications.
5. Technical constraints
5.1 Performance. The solution must provide response times compatible with daily clinical use. As an indication, a response time below 2 seconds is expected for common queries. A high level of answer relevance is expected.
5.2 Availability. Continuity of care requires a high level of availability, including outside office hours (24/7 services). An availability target of the order of 99.9% is expected.
5.3 Integration and interoperability. The solution must integrate with the existing hospital information system (SIH) and the electronic patient record (DPI). It must support health interoperability standards (HL7 / FHIR), the national health identifier (INS), and the national framework (Ségur du numérique en santé, Mon espace santé / DMP). API-type interfaces are expected, as is integration with user directories.
6. Sovereignty constraints
The authority attaches particular importance to data sovereignty. Hosting must be performed on SecNumCloud-qualified infrastructure or equivalent, and particular attention will be paid to dependency on non-European suppliers.
7. Budget constraints
The global budget allocated to the project is estimated at 900,000 over 5 years. The annual operating cost must remain controlled and compatible with the authority's budgetary capacity. The titleholder must propose an approach that optimises costs while guaranteeing the expected quality of service.
8. Environmental constraints
As part of its sustainable-development policy, CHRB wishes to limit the environmental footprint of its information systems. The titleholder must propose a solution that contributes to reducing energy consumption and relies, as far as possible, on low-carbon infrastructure.
9. Deployment terms
The project must be conducted progressively, with a pilot phase on a limited perimeter (one or two departments) followed by progressive generalisation to all services. Change-management support and staff training are expected.
10. Evaluation criteria
Offers will be evaluated against the following criteria: quality and relevance of the proposed solution; compliance with regulatory requirements; level of security; data sovereignty; quality of integration with the existing SIH/DPI; total cost of the solution; consideration of environmental issues; capacity for innovation.
